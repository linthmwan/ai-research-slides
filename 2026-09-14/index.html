Late-to-Early Training

LET LLMs learn earlier - then train faster and better

Ji Zhao et al. - HKUST (Guangzhou) and ByteDance Seed
ICLR 2026 PosterarXiv:2602.05393
Source: Abstract p.1; Fig. 1 p.2 | Deck: 2026-09-14 (paper announced 2026-02-05)
THE ONE-SENTENCE IDEA

Use a small pretrained model's final representation to shape an early layer of a larger model - only while the large model is still learning its foundations.

This is not output-logit KD: the target retains later layers to refine the borrowed signal.
Source: Abstract p.1; Methodology pp.3-4
WHY THIS PROBLEM EXISTS

Pretraining throws away a useful asset: prior training compute

  • A 12B LLM can require about 72,000 A100 GPU-hours to pretrain.
  • Open-source small models embody already-paid-for optimization, but are usually used only for inference or fine-tuning.
  • Conventional KD asks a smaller student to imitate a larger teacher - the opposite direction from scaling a new target.
Question: can a weak-but-mature model accelerate a larger model without constraining its final capability?
Source: Introduction pp.1-2
WHAT PRIOR ROUTES MISS

A small teacher needs a different integration point

Classical KD

  • Align output distributions.
  • Large teacher adds memory/compute.
  • A substantially smaller teacher can bottleneck the student.

Model growth / data selection

  • Change depth/width or curate data.
  • Needs architecture-specific design or preprocessing.
  • Does not directly reuse hidden-state knowledge from released models.
Source: Introduction p.2; Related Work pp.18-19
PAPER CLAIMS

Four deliverables, not just a training recipe

  1. A general late-to-early pretraining paradigm for a target up to 10x larger than its guide.
  2. Two mechanisms: late-to-early layer alignment and late-to-early step scheduling.
  3. Evidence across 1.4B and 7B LLaMA-style models, multiple small-model families and vocabularies.
  4. Ablations, resource accounting, a curvature argument, and a documented failure case.
Source: Introduction p.3; §3 and Appendix pp.5-27
METHOD / PIPELINE

During early steps, train M normally and add one representation constraint

  • Forward the same minibatch through target M and frozen pretrained T.
  • Read an early hidden state from M and the final hidden state from T.
  • If widths differ, project/interpolate M into T's width.
  • L2-normalize both representations.
  • Add negative cosine similarity to causal NLL.
  • Linearly decay the auxiliary coefficient to zero at S_stop.
Ltotal = LNLL + lambda(s) Lproj
Source: Algorithm 1 and Eqs. 3-5, Methodology p.4; Appendix F p.24
METHOD / TWO AXES

The name is a precise design choice

Late -> early layer

  • T's final layer carries mature features.
  • Align it to an early layer of M.
  • Later M layers remain free to rework it.

Late -> early step

  • A pretrained T stands in for a later training phase.
  • Its guidance is strongest when M is immature.
  • lambda decays to zero before T becomes a ceiling.
Source: Methodology pp.3-4; §3.3 pp.7-8
MECHANISM / CROWN PAGE

L2E works because a weak teacher's information enters before the target's remaining transformation stack - so the target can adapt and refine it instead of being pinned to it.

The proposal is an interface design: guidance at an early representation boundary, autonomy afterward.
Source: §3.3 pp.7-8
EXPERIMENT DESIGN

A practical pretraining comparison

  • Targets: 1.4B and 7B LLaMA-style models; RMSNorm + SwiGLU; BF16.
  • Data: The Pile, about 20B tokens; batch 2048; sequence length 1280.
  • Hardware: 32 NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs.
  • Baselines: standard causal LM, SALT, reverse KD (RKD).
  • Evaluation: nine one-shot downstream tasks plus held-out The Pile perplexity.
  • Small guides: SmolLM, OPT, Pythia; a 1.4B target uses guides around 125-160M.
Source: §3.1 pp.5-6

RESULT / CONVERGENCE

The headline speedup is a convergence result

For 1.4B, LET at 67% of total steps reaches 42.5 average accuracy, already above the fully trained 41.6 baseline.

RESULT / LANGUAGE MODELING

The effect is not tied to one tokenizer or one small-model family

  • LET lowers test perplexity in all three reported vocabulary settings.
  • The tested small guides are approximately 125-160M; SmolLM gives the strongest overall result among them.
Source: Fig. 2 and §3.2 p.6
ABLATION / WHERE TO ALIGN

Final-T -> early-M is the winning alignment geometry

  • Six variants move the source between middle/final T and the target between early/middle/late M.
  • Final-layer T to early-layer M (L2E) gives the highest downstream performance and lowest final perplexity.
  • Non-L2E choices show post-alignment perplexity increases; the remaining layers matter as an adaptation buffer.
Source: Fig. 3-4 and §3.3 pp.7-8
ABLATION / HOW MUCH TO ALIGN

lambda = 0.1 balances transfer against learning from data

lambda > 0.1 over-aligns M to T; lambda = 0.01 helps but leaves guidance underused.
Source: Fig. 5-6 and §3.3 pp.8-9
THE TAKEAWAY FROM ABLATION

A small teacher helps only if it is treated as temporary representation scaffolding, not as a target to match as strongly and as long as possible.

Schedule, layer boundary, and teacher quality are all first-order choices.
Source: §3.3 pp.7-9; Appendix C/I pp.21, 27

ANALYSIS / WHY EARLY MAY BE SMOOTHER

The theory is limited, but it matches the design intuition

  • In a simplified deep linear network, the alignment loss affects parameters only before alignment depth k.
  • That makes the alignment Hessian block-sparse after k.
  • Under a uniform Hessian-block bound, the paper derives ||H_proj(k)||_F <= k C.
  • Earlier k therefore has a smaller curvature upper bound.
This is a bounded simplified-model argument, not a proof for full Transformers; the empirical layer ablation is the stronger evidence.
Source: Appendix H pp.24-26
ROBUSTNESS / CHOICE OF GUIDE

Modern small models work; weak or stale teachers can fail

  • Supplementary tests report improvements with OPT-125M, Pythia-160M, and Llama-3.2-1B guides.
  • GPT-2 Small and Medium guides underperform the 1.4B baseline: 40.7 and 41.1 versus 41.6 average accuracy.
Source: Appendix C p.20-22; Appendix I p.27
LIMITS

The practical envelope is still narrow

  • The core evidence covers 1.4B, 3B, and 7B targets trained on up to 20B tokens - not 70B+ or trillion-token pretraining.
  • Wall-clock throughput is slightly lower during early training, even if convergence is faster.
  • Teacher data/model quality is a real precondition; the GPT-2 failure case makes that explicit.
  • Reviewer records were not available from the identified OpenReview forum (HTTP 403), so this deck does not infer review scores.
Source: Discussion pp.9-10; Appendix I p.27; ingest record
ENGINEERING READ

When does LET look worth testing?

Good candidate

  • You already have a credible small pretrained guide.
  • The target is costly enough that convergence speed matters.
  • You can add/remove a hidden-state loss without changing the target architecture.

Validate first

  • Sweep lambda and S_stop; do not assume stronger alignment helps.
  • Ablate alignment depth for your architecture.
  • Measure wall-clock, VRAM, quality, and teacher quality - not just loss curves.
Source: Methodology p.4; §3.3 pp.7-9; Appendix E/I pp.23, 27
APPENDIX / PAPER IN ITS OWN WORDS

The core idea is to guide the early layers of an LLM during early training using representations from the late layers of a pretrained model.

重點不是讓大模型模仿小模型的輸出，而是在早期用小模型末層表徵引導大模型的早層。Abstract p.1

The subsequent layers retain sufficient capacity to adapt and refine these representations through the learning dynamics of training.

對齊放在早層，才保留後段層數重新加工弱教師訊號。§3.3 p.8

Source: Paper text, Abstract p.1; §3.3 p.8
APPENDIX / STOPPING IS PART OF THE METHOD

Continued alignment with a much smaller teacher model can actually hinder further improvement.

當學生的能力已經提高，繼續對齊遠小於它的教師反而會阻礙後續進步。Appendix C p.21

When employing GPT-2 as the small model T, LET underperforms the baseline.

教師品質不是可忽略的細節；使用 GPT-2 作為小模型時，LET 仍低於 baseline。Appendix I p.27
