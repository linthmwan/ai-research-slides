Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners

GenCeption: turning a pretrained text-to-video diffusion transformer into a prompt-steered, one-pass perception model.

Letian Wang et al. - Google DeepMind, University of Toronto, UCL, Oxford, MIT, Lund University
ECCV 2026 PosterarXiv:2607.09024
Source: cover p.1; Figure 3 p.5; official ECCV 2026 paper index
THE THESIS

Video generation can be the pretraining objective for general-purpose perception.

Keep the text-to-video backbone and its priors; replace 50-step sampling with one prompt-conditioned forward pass.

Abstract p.1; §1 p.2; §3.2 pp.6-7
PROBLEM

Vision foundation models still specialize by task

  • Architecture fragmentation: heads, decoders, and losses are commonly redesigned for each task.
  • Temporal gap: image-domain generalists do not natively model 4D dynamics.
  • Missing analogue: vision lacks a scalable, language-aligned counterpart to next-token prediction.
The target is not “one multitask model”; it is a task-agnostic pretraining and post-training recipe.
§1 pp.1-2; §2 pp.3-4
DESIGN CRITERIA

Why text-to-video generation is the proposed catalyst

  1. Spatiotemporal evolution: predict a moving world, not independent frames.
  2. Vision-language alignment: text conditioning grounds visual features in semantics.
  3. Scale: video generation can use broad data and compute without dense task labels.
Generation is valuable here for the priors it forces the backbone to internalize.
§1 pp.1-2
CONTRIBUTIONS

What GenCeption adds beyond a video generator

  1. Repurpose a pretrained text-to-video DiT as a unified perceiver.
  2. Use text instructions to select target modality in one architecture.
  3. Represent dense tasks visually and sparse tasks with learnable query tokens.
  4. Show multi-task results, synthetic-to-real transfer, and pretraining comparisons.
Abstract p.1; §1-3 pp.2-9

METHOD / CONVERSION

Convert diffusion into feed-forward perception

Input: clean video latent x0 + text prompt; timestep fixed at t = 0
  • Standard WAN iterates denoising; GenCeption runs one DiT forward pass.
  • The DiT velocity output is negated before decoding or loss.
DiT predicts v = epsilon - x0
Use -v = x0 - epsilon
The paper treats the final DiT layer as a rich, decoder-aligned feature extractor.
§3.2 pp.6-7
METHOD / REPRESENTATION

Make dense tasks look like visual generation

Dense outputs
  • Depth and segmentation: replicate a scalar across RGB channels.
  • Normals and DensePose: use three channels directly.
  • Camera pose: pack ray origin and direction into a spatial “Rothko” raymap.
Benefit
  • One backbone, one decoder, and no task-specific dense head.
  • Task customization migrates to target encoding and prompt language.
The task format is designed to match the pretrained visual domain.
§3.3 p.7; Figure 5 p.7
METHOD / SPARSE OUTPUTS

Coordinates need tokens, not pixels

Token extension
  • Append one learnable token per video frame.
  • A small MLP maps each token to K-dimensional 2D/3D coordinate targets.
  • Use native 3D RoPE and temporally interpolate positions.
Trade-off exposed later
  • It enables structured outputs unavailable in RGB.
  • But scratch-trained tokens deviate from pixel-space pretraining and can disturb attention.
§3.3 p.7; §4.5 pp.12-13
METHOD / DATA

Synthetic video supplies dense ground truth at scale

800 assets200 motions7,500 videos
  • RenderPeople humans are animated with CMU motions, varied scenes/HDRIs, and varied cameras.
  • Separate Blender passes produce normals, depth, masks, and 2D/3D keypoint supervision.
  • Extra synthetic depth/camera data includes TartanAir, Virtual KITTI, and MVS Synth.
§3.4 pp.8-9; §4.1 p.9
METHOD / OBJECTIVE

One L2 loss - specialization moves into target design

Dense tasks: L2 in latent space
Sparse tasks: L2 in output space
  • Joint multi-task training does not need manually balanced task losses.
Depth encoding: d_prime = clip(alpha log(d + 1), 0, 1)
  • Median-normalize each depth map, then map it into RGB-range targets.
  • Scale ambiguity is addressed in representation rather than loss selection.

LIMIT / IMPORTANT COUNTEREVIDENCE

Joint training is not uniformly better

What improves or holds
  • Foreground segmentation consistently benefits from joint training.
  • Referring segmentation remains largely unchanged.
  • Some dense tasks retain performance or regress by benchmark.
What breaks
  • 3D human-keypoint estimation degrades severely.
  • Authors point to token-coordinate regression and scratch tokens disrupting pretrained attention.
  • Implication: preserve native backbone behavior where possible.
§4.5 pp.12-13
ENGINEERING TAKEAWAYS

Three practical lessons for multimodal systems

  1. Keep the pretraining contract: use inputs, output formats, and computations that look native to the backbone.
  2. Prompts can replace routing: a task label in text is a simpler interface than branching model heads.
  3. Test generality adversarially: a shared dense representation may scale, while sparse structured outputs expose interference.
This paper is most convincing as a post-training design argument: representation choices decide whether generative priors remain usable.
§3.2-3.5 pp.6-9; §4.5 pp.12-13
