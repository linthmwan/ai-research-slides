There is No VAE

End-to-End Pixel-Space Generative Modeling via Self-Supervised Pre-Training

Jiachen Lei, Keli Liu, Julius Berner, Haiming Yu, Hongkai Zheng, Jiahong Wu, Xiangxiang Chu · AMAP, Alibaba Group / Caltech
ICLR 2026 PosterarXiv:2510.12586Announced 2026-01-26
Source: paper cover / OpenReview ICLR 2026 Poster
TL;DR

Pixel-space diffusion was not doomed by pixels; it was missing noise-consistent semantic pretraining.

EPG first trains an encoder to preserve semantics along the same diffusion trajectory, then fine-tunes encoder+decoder end-to-end for generation.

§1 p.2-3; Fig.2 p.4
Problem

The VAE made latent diffusion efficient, but also fixed its ceiling

  • A pretrained VAE is hard to train because it must balance compression and high-fidelity reconstruction.
  • Even a good VAE creates a fixed-capacity bottleneck: the generator can only adapt within that representation.
  • Pixel-space training removes the VAE but historically pays with backbone cost or slow convergence.
  • So the real target is not “pixels vs latents”; it is how to get semantics before denoising.
EPG attacks the representation bottleneck, not just the architecture.
§1 p.2
Failure mode

Plain SSL collapses on heavily noised diffusion inputs

Why standard SSL is not enough
  • Augmented clean views teach semantics near the data manifold.
  • Diffusion training asks the encoder to handle points far along a noise schedule.
  • Direct SSL pretraining becomes ineffective because representation collapse appears under strong noise.
EPG’s reframing
  • Use deterministic PF-ODE trajectories as the geometry for positives / negatives.
  • Same trajectory means same semantic endpoint; different trajectory means different sample identity.
  • Semantics become temporally consistent across noise levels.
§1 p.2-3; §3.2 p.5
Contributions

What the paper actually adds

  1. A two-stage End-to-end Pixel-space Generative model: pretrain encoder, then attach a fresh decoder and fine-tune.
  2. Representation Consistency Learning: contrastive semantics + PF-ODE trajectory alignment.
  3. SOTA pixel-space ImageNet generation: FID 1.58 at 256 and 2.35 at 512.
  4. A strong one-step pixel-space consistency model without pretrained VAE or pretrained diffusion model: FID 8.82.
  5. Training efficiency: EPG-XL/16 beats DiT-XL/2 using 139h vs 506h on 8xH200.
§1 p.3; Tab.1 p.7; Tab.2 p.8; Tab.4 p.8; Tab.5 p.9
Method overview

Two stages: learn semantics first, generate pixels second

  • Projector is used only during pretraining and discarded before generation training.
  • Decoder starts randomly initialized, so final generator is not constrained by a pretrained VAE decoder.
Fig.2 p.4; §3.3 p.6
Core objective

Representation Consistency Learning = clean-view SSL + trajectory SSL

L = NT-Xent(Lθ(Eθ(y1)), Lθ-(Eθ-(y2))) + NT-Xent(Eθ(xtn), Esg(θ)(xtn-1))
  • Contrastive term: augmented clean views are positives; batch samples are negatives.
  • Trajectory term: adjacent points on the same PF-ODE path are positives.
  • Adjacent points from different trajectories become negatives, so semantics stay separable under noise.
Eq.7-8 p.5; §3.2 p.5
Stabilization

Temperature controls how tightly noisy points are forced to align

τ(t) = τ1(1 - t) + τ2t, with τ1=0.1 and τ2=0.2
  • Small τ gives stronger separability and tighter same-trajectory alignment.
  • Early training uses looser alignment for large-noise samples because the encoder has not yet learned meaningful features.
  • The schedule is independent of brittle EMA hyperparameter coupling in the earlier rRCM framework.
The schedule makes noisy-semantics learning trainable without hand-tuned collapse-prone coupling.
§3.2 p.5-6; Tab.10 p.16
Fine-tuning

Same pretrained encoder supports diffusion and one-step consistency

Diffusion model
  • Denoising objective in pixel space.
  • EDM-style SDE and x-prediction.
  • Main runs: batch 1024, 1M/2M steps on ImageNet-256.
Consistency model
  • Trains with consistency objective plus auxiliary semantic loss.
  • Auxiliary target uses frozen pretrained encoder Wφ, not an external model.
  • Reported as one-step ImageNet-256 generation.
§3.3 p.6; Tab.9 p.15; Tab.11 p.16
Architecture detail

Keep token length stable by scaling patch size with resolution

  • ImageNet-256 uses 16x16 patches; ImageNet-512 uses 32x32 patches.
  • Encoder/decoder blocks are separated by configuration; XL and larger models enlarge decoder width.
  • The design keeps GFLOPs low when scaling resolution.

Scaling

Downstream generation improves with pretraining budget and encoder size

  • Batch size scales from 256 to 1024 in the first two columns.
  • Encoder scales from 64M to 107M to 225M; decoder scales accordingly during fine-tuning.
  • This supports the authors’ argument that the remaining gap to leading latent models is partly compute-limited.

Mechanism crown

The key object is not the pixel, but the trajectory-stable semantic identity behind the pixel.

EPG makes two noisy samples positive only when they belong to the same deterministic path back to the same clean image.

Limitations

The paper is honest about where pixel-space still loses

  • EPG currently underperforms leading latent-space REPA and RAE on the headline ImageNet-256 table.
  • The authors argue the gap is largely due to training compute disparity, not a fundamental limit.
  • The method still requires large GPU budgets: e.g. 57h pretraining plus 139h or 160h diffusion fine-tuning on 8xH200 for XL/XXL.
  • Evidence is ImageNet-centric; high-resolution multi-modal or open-domain generation is left as future work.
This is a strong argument against the VAE bottleneck, not a claim that latent diffusion is obsolete tomorrow.
§6 p.10; Appendix B.4 p.15; Tab.1 p.7
Engineering read

What to steal for image-generation systems

  1. Treat generator encoders as representation learners first, denoisers second.
  2. Define positives using generative trajectories, not only data augmentation.
  3. Let the encoder update during pixel reconstruction; frozen semantics are too high-level.
  4. Measure total system cost: VAE pretraining, generator fine-tuning, NFE, and GFLOPs all change the conclusion.
  5. For one-step models, reuse the pretrained encoder as internal semantic supervision instead of relying on a separate teacher model.

Appendix highlight

The key decomposition, in the authors’ words

“we argue encoders in diffusion-based generative models primarily learn high-level visual semantics from noisy inputs and the decoders act as low-level pixel generators conditioned on encoder representations.”

作者把生成模型拆成「噪聲輸入上的語意編碼器」與「條件化像素生成解碼器」；EPG 的兩階段訓練就是從這個判斷長出來的。§1 p.2

“The representation consistency loss uses temporally adjacent points along the ODE trajectory as positive pairs … while adjacent points from different trajectories serve as negatives.”
