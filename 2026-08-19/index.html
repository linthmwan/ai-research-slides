CubeComposer: Spatio-Temporal Autoregressive 4K 360° Video Generation from Perspective Video

A CVPR 2026 paper that turns perspective-to-360° video into cubemap face × temporal-window autoregressive diffusion.

Lingen Li, Guangzhi Wang, Xiaoyu Li, Zhaoyang Zhang, Qi Dou, Jinwei Gu, Tianfan Xue, Ying Shan · CUHK + ARC Lab, Tencent PCG
arXiv:2603.04291Accepted to CVPR 2026Native 4K 360°
Source: arXiv:2603.04291v1, cover / Fig.1 p.1; arXiv comments: Accepted to CVPR 2026

The key move is not a bigger video DiT; it is changing the sequence: generate one cubemap face over one temporal window, with context.

That converts a 4K 360° video from one memory-heavy full-attention denoising problem into a planned spatio-temporal AR process.
Abstract p.1; §1 pp.1–3; Fig.2 p.2
Problem

Native 4K is where previous perspective-to-360° systems hit the wall

  • VR wants 3840 × 1920 equirectangular video; prior methods run natively at ≤1K because full-attention diffusion over the whole panorama is too expensive.
  • A post SR model such as VEnhancer can upsample to 2K, but the paper argues this lacks intrinsic generative reasoning and can amplify artifacts.
  • Perspective input is partial and rotating, so the generator must both preserve observed regions and hallucinate unseen regions consistently.
The paper’s target is not just sharper frames; it is native high-resolution generation with geometry and motion coherence.
§1 pp.1–2; Fig.1 p.1
Why temporal models alone are insufficient

For 360° video, autoregression has a spatial axis too

Temporal AR

  • Long-video methods mainly decide which future frames to generate next.
  • History context is the natural conditioning signal.

Spatio-temporal AR

  • CubeComposer must decide which cube face and which time window to generate.
  • Sparse future observations from the perspective video are useful context, not forbidden leakage.
§2.3 p.3; §3.3–3.4 pp.5–6
Contributions

The paper stacks four engineering choices around one representation choice

  1. Cubemap representation and face-window AR generation for native 4K 360° output.
  2. Coverage-guided order planning tied to the input camera trajectory.
  3. History + current + future-fragment context with sparse context attention.
  4. Cube-aware positional encoding, padding, and blending to suppress seams.
  5. 4K360Vid: 11,832 high-resolution 360° clips with global and face-wise captions.
§1 p.3; §4.1 p.6

Representation

Cubemap avoids equirectangular distortion and creates six controllable subproblems

  • Input perspective frames with camera rotations are projected into masked equirectangular video, then converted to six cubemap faces F/R/B/L/U/D.
  • Each step synthesizes one face over a fixed temporal window, then the completed cubemap video is assembled back into 4K equirectangular output.
  • The representation matches existing foundation-model priors better than directly denoising the distorted equirectangular frame.
(Xcondf,t, Mf,t) = Πcube(Iperst; φ, Rt)
§3.1 pp.3–4
Order planning

Generate the most observed face first, then let evidence propagate

cf,w = (1/Twin) Σ mean(Mf,t)
σw = argsortf∈F(-cf,w)
  • Temporal windows remain causal to preserve time consistency.
  • Within a window, faces are sorted by descending coverage from the input perspective video.
  • High-certainty faces reduce early uncertainty and pass geometry / appearance / motion cues to later faces.
§1 p.2; §3.3 p.5
Context mechanism

Context is a three-part memory, not just previous frames

  • History tokens: up to H previously generated windows.
  • Current-window tokens: generated faces plus perspective conditions for ungenerated faces.
  • Future fragments: nearest valid conditional segments from current or adjacent faces above threshold r.
§3.4 pp.5–6; Fig.4 p.4
Context formula

The future fragment is chosen by coverage, not by a fixed frame index

uw,f = [uhistw; ucurrw,f; ufutw,f]
τ*g = arg minτ ≥ ew { τ | c̄g(τ; Tfrag) ≥ r }
  • The selected future fragments come from the current face and adjacent faces N(f).
  • This gives the generator temporal foresight when the perspective video later observes the region being hallucinated now.
  • It is a 360°-specific difference from pure causal next-frame generation.
§3.4 pp.5–6; Eq.5–8

Sparse context attention is the enabling trick: keep context, remove the C² blow-up.

Generation tokens fully self-attend; context tokens attend to generation and only locally to themselves with a diagonal-banded mask.
full: O((G+C)²)SCA: O(G² + G·C + K·C)
§3.4 p.6; Fig.3 p.4
Seam control

Cubemap AR creates boundaries; topology-aware generation repairs them

  • Positions are remapped according to flattened cubemap topology, not each face’s isolated tensor layout.
  • Current-face latents are padded with rotated/flipped strips from adjacent faces.
  • After decoding, overlaps are blended back into adjacent faces to smooth boundary transitions.
§3.5 p.6; Fig.5 p.6
Training

The model reuses Wan 2.2 5B priors and trains on simulated perspective views

  • Training samples a temporal window and cube face, constructs the §3.4 context, and predicts velocity with flow matching.
  • Random perspective trajectories use 3–5 anchor points and FoV 60–120°.
  • Global prompts and optional face-wise prompts teach the model controllability in regions not covered by the input perspective video.
L = E || vθ(zt, t; uw,f, y) − vt ||2

Mechanism insight: CubeComposer uses observation strength to define causality.

Instead of an arbitrary face order, coverage from the moving perspective camera determines which part of the panorama becomes reliable context for the next parts.
§3.3 p.5; §4.3 p.8
Limits

The next bottleneck is serving, not just quality

  • The conclusion names reducing diffusion steps and moving toward streaming 360° generation as future directions.
  • The main paper compresses extra analysis and user studies into the supplementary.
  • Fair comparison is complicated by baselines supporting different camera-rotation ranges and native resolutions.
For product deployment, the story after 4K is latency: can the face/window AR loop become streamable?
§5 p.8; §4.2–4.3 pp.6–8
Engineering read

What to borrow for other temporal generation systems

  1. Turn geometry into scheduling: use input coverage/observability to decide generation order.
  2. Separate context composition from attention sparsification: first decide what evidence matters, then make it cheap.
  3. For multi-view outputs, handle seams as first-class model design, not only post-processing.
  4. Treat future conditional fragments as valid evidence when the task is completion from a known input sequence.

Appendix · abstract highlight

Why the cubemap AR decomposition matters

By decomposing videos into cubemap representations with six faces, CubeComposer autoregressively synthesizes content in a well-planned spatio-temporal order, reducing memory demands while enabling high-resolution output.
