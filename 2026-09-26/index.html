The Alignment Illusion
in MLLMs

Why high visual-text similarity can survive after the image content is gone.

Hong-Han Wang · Yuntao Wang · Hu Ding
University of Science and Technology of China
NeurIPS 2026 acceptedarXiv:2609.30210v1 · 2026-09-24
Full paper title: The Alignment Illusion in Multimodal Large Language Models · Abstract p.1
TL;DR

High internal alignment is not proof that an MLLM uses the image.

Shared MLP geometry can make visual and text states look aligned even when visual content is corrupted.
Abstract p.1; §1 p.2
THE INTERPRETATION PEOPLE MAKE

A rising similarity curve is commonly read as progressive visual-text integration.

  • Layer-wise CKA, SVCCA, MIR, and leading principal-angle cosine compare hidden visual/text states.
  • In independent networks, high similarity can support “similar features.”
  • But projector-LLM MLLMs send both streams through the same Transformer weights.
The independence assumption breaks before the interpretation begins.
§1–2, pp.1–3
THE FAILURE MODE

Alignment illusion = scalar similarity remains high after content is removed.

  • Content-induced alignment: representations couple because the image helps answer the question.
  • Weight-induced alignment: shared weights impose common geometry regardless of visual content.

A scalar score collapses both sources into one number.

§1 p.2; Fig. 1 p.2
CONTROLLED TEST

Break the image-question relationship while holding token scale under control.

  1. ORIG: projector output from the matched image.
  2. NOISE: Gaussian visual tokens, rescaled to each original token norm.
  3. IRR: natural image from another question; SHUF: permuted ORIG tokens; TEXT: no visual tokens.
13 MLLMs5 families0.5B–72B1,000 MMBench Qs
§3.1 p.4; §4.1 p.7; App. A p.12
RESULT 1 / CONTENT REMOVAL

Accuracy collapses; four scalar alignment measures do not reliably separate ORIG from NOISE.

The leading cosine can even match or exceed ORIG after visual content is destroyed.
§3.1 p.4; Fig. 2 p.4
WHERE THE ILLUSION IS MADE

The shared MLP pathway, not merely attention, amplifies apparent alignment.

§3.2; Fig. 3 p.5
MECHANISM / CROWN PAGE

A shared anisotropic down-projection pulls both modalities toward the same dominant output direction.

That creates alignment without requiring any content-level coupling between image and text.
§3.2, pp.5–6; Proposition 1 p.6
MECHANISM EVIDENCE

Four empirical steps connect the high score to trained Wout geometry.

  1. Near-unity layer-wise visual/text alignment appears only after both streams enter the shared LLM path.
  2. Bypassing MLP changes sigma1 3.5x more than bypassing attention (median, all 13 models).
  3. The Wout top-two singular-value ratio is 1.2–1.6, above a matched random reference in every model.
  4. PA basis directions capture 2.8x–13.6x more top-10 Wout-subspace energy than random directions.
§3.2 p.5; Fig. 3 p.5
WHY ONE NUMBER FAILS

sigma1 sees the strongest shared axis and misses whether alignment occupies the remaining subspace.

Delta_PA(l) = sigma1(l) - sigma2(l)
  • Small PA gap: multiple shared directions; structured visual information remains.
  • Large PA gap: alignment collapses mostly to the Wout-induced dominant axis.

THE HARDER CONTROL

Irrelevant images preserve visual structure but sever task relevance.

  • IRR is a real image sampled from another question.
  • If the model ignores it, IRR should match TEXT.
  • Instead, IRR scores below TEXT: the model actively processes distractor content.
IRR−TEXT: -5.0 ppSHUF−ORIG: -0.8 pp
§4.1 p.7; Table 2 p.8; Fig. 6 p.9
WHERE IRRELEVANT CONTENT TRAVELS

The projector encodes IRR; the same PA subspace carries its influence into the LLM.

  • Linear probes recover irrelevant-image categories at 74.4%–78.5% versus 5% chance.
  • In-band PA-subspace perturbations hurt accuracy more than matched orthogonal perturbations in all 13 models.
  • The in-band excess is 1.1–5.5 pp: this subspace is causal transport, not merely a similarity visualization.
§4.2; Fig. 5 p.8

INTERPRETATION

PA gap is a diagnostic of visual-stream geometry, not a certificate of task-relevant multimodal reasoning.

Report controlled task evidence alongside internal geometry — neither can replace the other.
§4.3 p.9; §5 pp.9–10
PRACTICAL READING GUIDE

What a careful MLLM-analysis paper should report.

  1. Use interventions that preserve low-level scale while changing content or relevance.
  2. Inspect the PA spectrum, not only the leading similarity scalar.
  3. Test whether the suspected subspace causally affects outputs.
  4. Pair representation geometry with accuracy under the same interventions.
“Aligned” needs a mechanism and a counterfactual, not just a high curve.
§1 p.2; §3–5, pp.4–10
LIMITS / SCOPE

The conclusion is deliberately narrower than “all multimodal alignment is invalid.”

  • Studied architecture: projector-LLM designs with a shared language-model pathway.
  • Studied task: controlled multiple-choice MMBench evaluation and projector-output interventions.
  • Unresolved domains: other fusion designs, open-ended generation, video, documents, and agentic tasks.
The paper argues for calibrated interpretation — not for discarding internal geometry.
§5 p.10

APPENDIX / PROTOCOL

Evaluation controls that make the intervention claim credible

  • Fixed subset of 1,000 MMBench questions across every model and intervention.
  • NOISE is norm-matched per visual token; IRR uses the same fixed pairing across models.
  • PCA top-k=30; alpha mix has 11 steps; bootstrap accuracy intervals use 1,000 resamples; seed=42.
